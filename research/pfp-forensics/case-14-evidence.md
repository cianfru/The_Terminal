# PART 2 — Clustering the operator

## The repo's own fund/drain rule FAILS on this wallet. Do not use its output.

Running `clusterEntities`' rules as written (FUND = a wallet empty beforehand seeded **≥50,000 SPX**;
DRAIN = a wallet emptying ≥90% into another) from `0xce9a1739` produced **63 wallets and 116 links**,
and sprawled into a completely unrelated 2023 neighbourhood that the subject never touched. The repo's
own SIZE guard says anything over 30 wallets is flagged and never trusted, so this output is rejected.

**Root cause: the threshold is denominated in TOKENS, not value.**

| date | SPX price | what 50,000 SPX was worth |
|---|---|---|
| 2023-09-04 | $0.00216 | **$108** |
| 2023-12-20 | $0.00609 | $305 |
| 2024-05-17 | $0.00691 | $346 |
| 2025-07-28 (ATH) | $2.13940 | **$106,970** |
| 2026-09-21 | $0.48341 | $24,170 |

SPX has moved ~1,000× since launch. In the launch era 50,000 SPX was a **$108 tip**, and the community
was passing round meme amounts — 69,420, 111,111, 123,456 — constantly. Every one of those registered as
"seeding a fresh wallet." The rule was calibrated on present-day whale behaviour and silently inverts in
the early history. **This is a live defect in the production clustering engine, not just in this study.**

## The cluster, on a value-denominated rule

**TIER A — publishable.** A wallet empties **≥90% of its balance INTO a wallet that held nothing
beforehand**, worth **≥$5,000 at the time**. Both conditions, not either. That is a wallet migration and
is very hard to explain as a payment, a sale or a gift.

Result: **3 wallets, 2 links.** The crawl terminates on its own — it does not hit any guard.

## A second engine defect found on the way: EIP-7702 breaks the is-contract cache

`0xc41e7f22` returns code today: `0xef010063c0c19a282a1b52b07dd5a65b58948a07dae32b`. The `0xef0100`
prefix is an **EIP-7702 delegation designator** — an EOA that opted into a smart account. EIP-7702
shipped with Pectra in **May 2025**, so code present today says nothing about 2023–24; this address was
a plain EOA when it received the drain.

`enrich-addr-types.mjs` describes its cache as "monotonic, immutable" — an address that is a contract
stays a contract. **EIP-7702 breaks that assumption in the other direction**: an address that was an EOA
for its whole history now reads as a contract, so every historical link through it is silently dropped.
Any account whose code starts `0xef0100` should be treated as an EOA.

sale." It reported the subject as having **sold 2,424,642 SPX**. The careful transaction-level
reconstruction says **839,674**. The cheap method is wrong twice, in opposite directions:

- it counted the **1,984,968 LP mint** as a sale (those tokens came back two days later), and
- it **missed every aggregator sell** — CoW, 0x, 1inch, Paraswap route through a settlement contract,
  so the tokens never appear to move to a pool. The 400,000 CoW sale vanished entirely.

Both errors are invisible unless you open the transaction. Numbers below are the transaction-level ones.

## The safe claim

A three-wallet operator bought 7.2M SPX on the open market in September–October 2023, **sold only 30%
of it**, moved the other 70% out to addresses that cannot be linked to it under a value-denominated
fund/drain rule, and holds nothing today.

**What CANNOT be claimed:** that the 5.1M transferred out was sold, or kept, or that it went to the same
person. It left the confirmed cluster and the trail stops there. `0xc41e7f22` — which received 2M of it
and has sold 2.18M into pools — is a *candidate*, not a member, because a second wallet funded it the
same day. And no part of this establishes who any of these wallets belong to.
